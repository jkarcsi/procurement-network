# Procura — Project One-Pager

## 1. Short Description

**Procura** is an intelligent B2B request-for-quotation and supplier network for Hungarian small and medium-sized enterprises. It helps companies turn a short, informal procurement need into a structured, targeted and comparable request for quotations from relevant suppliers.

The goal of the first version is not to become a full ERP system, a complete procurement department or an enterprise management platform. Instead, Procura focuses on solving one specific problem well:

**how a Hungarian SME can move from an uncertain, manual quotation process to receiving multiple comparable, decision-ready offers quickly and efficiently.**

## 2. The Problem

Many Hungarian SMEs do not have a professional procurement process. When they need to find a service provider or supplier, the process is often ad hoc and inefficient:

* they ask acquaintances for recommendations;
* they search for a few companies on Google;
* they write emails manually;
* they receive offers in different formats;
* prices, conditions and service scopes are difficult to compare;
* there is no well-documented decision trail;
* supplier performance is not measurable over time;
* the process takes a lot of time but does not necessarily lead to a better decision.

This is especially painful for recurring external service purchases where the company is not making a one-time purchase, but choosing a service provider that may become a regular operating cost.

## 3. Target Group

The primary target group:

**Hungarian SMEs with 10–100 employees that regularly use external service providers but do not have a dedicated procurement team or a well-defined procurement system.**

Typical users include:

* managing directors;
* operations managers;
* office managers;
* finance or administrative staff;
* site managers;
* employees responsible for smaller-scale procurement tasks.

The other side of the marketplace:

**local and national service-provider SMEs** that want to acquire new business customers but do not have a mature sales system, lead generation engine or strong online presence.

## 4. The Solution

Procura guides users through the core quotation-request process:

1. The buyer briefly describes what they need.
2. The system identifies the procurement category the request belongs to, or records it as a potential category expansion need if no suitable category exists.
3. The buyer can review and modify the suggested category.
4. The system asks clarification questions to help create a better request for quotation.
5. The informal need is converted into a structured RFQ.
6. Based on category, region and supplier data, the system can suggest relevant suppliers.
7. The buyer can expand the supplier list with their own invited suppliers.
8. Suppliers can respond in a simple way, potentially without full registration for invited requests, while registration may be required for tracking participation or applying to open opportunities.
9. Before submitting an offer, suppliers can ask public clarification questions that are visible to other participating suppliers.
10. Buyers can answer clarification questions and, if necessary, modify or withdraw the RFQ.
11. Incoming offers are displayed in a more standardized and comparable format.
12. The system can rank anonymous offers based on selected criteria such as price, delivery time, additional services, warranty or other relevant factors.
13. The buyer can compare offers and make an informed decision.
14. Decisions, status changes and important events are logged and remain traceable.

Procura is therefore not just a simple lead generation site. It provides a **structured procurement workflow for companies that currently do not have such a system in place.**

## 5. Core Value Proposition

### For Buyers

Procura helps buyers request quotations faster, more transparently and with a better decision-making basis.

Main benefits:

* less manual work;
* better-quality and more targeted RFQs;
* access to more relevant suppliers;
* more comparable offers;
* a traceable decision process;
* searchable procurement history for future reference.

### For Suppliers

Procura brings new and relevant business opportunities to suppliers that want more corporate customers but do not want to maintain expensive marketing or sales infrastructure.

Main benefits:

* more targeted RFQs;
* fewer irrelevant inquiries;
* simple offer submission;
* increased visibility through supplier profiles;
* potential for reputation building, ratings and recurring customer acquisition.

## 6. Why Now?

SMEs are under increasing pressure to reduce costs, increase transparency and automate operational processes. At the same time, the rise of AI-based tools makes it possible to partially automate and simplify workflows that previously required expert input, such as writing a high-quality RFQ or comparing multiple supplier offers.

The opportunity behind Procura is that it is not a generic chatbot or a simple form builder. It supports a specific business workflow:

**need → clarification → RFQ → supplier response → comparison → decision → learning data asset.**

## 7. Initial Focus / Beachhead Market

The initial focus should not be every procurement category. Procura should begin with local B2B service categories where:

* there are many SME buyers and suppliers;
* there are enough potential suppliers;
* the need is recurring;
* it is difficult to request high-quality quotations quickly;
* offers are hard to compare in terms of quality, scope and content;
* regional or local availability matters.

Suggested initial categories:

* cleaning services;
* air conditioning / HVAC;
* security services;
* occupational safety;
* fire safety;
* IT support.

The first market is Hungary, with a Hungarian-language interface tailored to the local business environment, while keeping future international expansion possible.

## 8. MVP Goal

The goal of the MVP is not to become a complete marketplace or ERP system. The goal is to prove that the core workflow works.

The MVP is successful if:

1. a buyer can create an RFQ within a few minutes;
2. the system turns the initial need into a better and more structured RFQ;
3. relevant suppliers receive the request;
4. at least some suppliers respond;
5. the buyer can compare the received offers;
6. a real business decision is made at the end of the process;
7. at least one side of the marketplace shows willingness to pay.

## 9. Main Features of the First Version

### Buyer Side

* registration and login;
* company profile;
* RFQ creation from a short text description;
* clarification questions;
* structured RFQ generation;
* supplier shortlist;
* RFQ sending;
* clarification questions and answers;
* viewing incoming offers;
* offer comparison;
* decision recording;
* basic audit trail.

### Supplier Side

* supplier profile;
* categories and service regions;
* viewing invited RFQs;
* simple offer submission;
* browsing open opportunities;
* response statistics.

### Admin Side

* user management;
* company and supplier management;
* RFQ review and moderation;
* category and region management;
* basic reports;
* identification of problematic or underserved categories and category expansion needs.

## 10. What Is Not Part of the First Version

The first version intentionally does not aim to include:

* a full ERP system;
* a complex enterprise procurement system;
* a public procurement platform;
* automatic contract signing;
* full financial settlement;
* escrow or payment infrastructure;
* coverage of every industry;
* international expansion;
* a native mobile-app-first strategy;
* fully automated decision-making.

Procura should not make decisions instead of the buyer. Its role is to provide a better basis for decision-making.

## 11. Business Model

Monetization should be introduced gradually.

Possible early revenue models:

### Buyer Subscription

Buyer companies pay a monthly fee for more RFQs, better comparison features, team functionality, exports and more advanced analysis.

### Credit-Based Usage

Certain analysis or premium features consume credits, such as detailed offer comparison or expanding a supplier shortlist.

### Supplier Premium Profile

Suppliers pay for better visibility, more categories, highlighted placement, a more detailed profile or access to more RFQ opportunities.

### Later Revenue Models

* qualified lead fees;
* success fees after completed deals;
* verified supplier badges;
* market benchmark reports;
* aggregated and anonymized market insights;
* partner or white-label models.

In the first stage, the goal is not to maximize monetization, but to prove willingness to pay.

## 12. Long-Term Vision

Procura can evolve in four stages:

1. **Procurement tool** — the buyer quickly creates a good RFQ and receives multiple offers.
2. **Marketplace** — buyers and suppliers can find each other independently.
3. **Network** — every transaction improves matching, reputation, pricing and reliability data.
4. **Infrastructure** — Procura can become a foundational layer for local B2B service procurement.

The long-term value is not only in the software, but also in the data asset that can be built over time:

* what companies request quotations for;
* which suppliers are active in which regions;
* what types of offers win;
* how prices develop;
* which suppliers respond quickly and reliably;
* which categories have supply gaps.

This data must only be used legally, ethically, in aggregated form and in a way that builds trust.

## 13. Main Risks

### Cold-Start Problem

Buyer demand and supplier supply must be built at the same time. If there are not enough suppliers, buyers will not receive good offers. If there are not enough buyers, suppliers will not return.

### Too Broad a Focus

If Procura tries to cover every industry, every region and every procurement type at once, it will build real marketplace liquidity too slowly.

### Lack of Trust

Buyers need to trust that they receive real and relevant suppliers. Suppliers need to trust that they receive genuine business opportunities, not spam.

### Data Protection and Legal Risk

Company data, contact data, offers, prices and business information are sensitive. Clear rules are needed from the beginning regarding data processing, deletion, opt-out options and legal basis.

### Premature Platform Expansion

The long-term vision can be ambitious, but the first version must prove one clearly measurable business workflow.

## 14. 90-Day Goal

The goal for the next 90 days:

* choose a narrow category and target-customer focus;
* conduct 20–30 buyer interviews;
* conduct 20–30 supplier interviews;
* collect at least 100 potential suppliers in the first selected categories;
* run 5–10 real RFQs, even if partly manually;
* test the working MVP with real companies;
* test willingness to pay on either the buyer or supplier side;
* refine product positioning and pricing.

## 15. Success Definition

The first stage of Procura can be considered successful if it proves business value, not only technical feasibility:

* at least 5–10 real RFQs are completed;
* each RFQ receives at least 2–3 relevant offers on average;
* buyers say the process is faster or better than their previous manual method;
* suppliers say the RFQs are relevant;
* at least some users would be willing to pay for the service;
* the first categories show where real marketplace liquidity can be built.

## 16. One-Sentence Strategic Summary

Procura’s first goal is to turn the chaotic, manual quotation-request process of Hungarian SMEs into a structured, comparable and traceable decision-making workflow — starting with narrow service categories and later expanding into a marketplace and procurement network.
